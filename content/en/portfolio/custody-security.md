At Cobo, as our institutional digital asset custody and WaaS (Wallet-as-a-Service) offerings scaled, we faced a critical challenge common to Web3 infrastructure: security is the ultimate non-negotiable baseline. How do you clearly, rigorously, and authoritatively articulate a deeply technical custody security model to current and prospective institutional clients?

As Lead Writer in a two-person team, I drove the end-to-end delivery of the bilingual *Digital Asset Security Manual*, a flagship publication totaling approximately 130,000 words. I personally authored and translated over half of the core content (~98,000 English words and ~31,000 Chinese characters).

---

## Establishing Boundaries and End-to-End Governance

When the project launched, the company already possessed various collateral—such as sales pitch decks and high-level product datasheets. This created ambiguity around the manual's precise positioning, target audience, and distinct value proposition. Furthermore, as the content delved into deep cryptographic and architectural concepts, alignment across engineering, product, and legal teams required structured management.

To keep a massive publication moving smoothly, I established a complete project lifecycle framework:

#### 1. Defining Purpose and Content Boundaries
I aligned closely with the Product Director to anchor the manual's core purpose: providing prospective institutional clients with an authoritative, technical deep-dive during security due diligence, while offering existing clients a comprehensive security reference. I explicitly differentiated it from sales collateral: pitch decks initiate the conversation, whereas this manual must withstand rigorous technical and compliance audits.

#### 2. Cross-Functional Outline & Timeline Alignment
After mapping stakeholder requirements, I drafted the core content hierarchy, project milestones, and delivery timeline. I conducted early alignment workshops across Product, Security, Core Engineering, Legal, and Compliance teams to secure cross-functional consensus on the scope before drafting began, preventing costly structural rework later.

#### 3. Phased Review Checkpoints & Quality Control
I divided writing responsibilities with my co-author and instituted structured review gates. To ensure technical precision, we adopted a 'Chinese-first technical review, followed by English localization' pipeline. Before final sign-off, we engaged select enterprise clients in a closed review phase to gather real-world feedback. Final sign-off from all department leads, alongside clear distribution guidelines, formally closed the editorial cycle.

#### 4. SPOC Communication Model
To streamline cross-team collaboration, I required each participating team (Engineering, Security, Legal) to designate a Single Point of Contact (SPOC). Asynchronous day-to-day clarifications were routed exclusively through these SPOCs, reserving full-team syncs for major milestone checkpoints. This disciplined communication model kept a 130,000-word initiative operating with high efficiency.

---

## Content Strategy & Executive Delivery

On production strategy and bilingual delivery, I focused on several core methodologies:

#### Risk-Centric Narrative over Feature Dumps
Rather than simply listing product features, the narrative framed security capabilities around real-world threat vectors and institutional risk scenarios. Simultaneously, I maintained strict security boundaries—abstracting and desensitizing sensitive infrastructure logic to prevent accidental exposure of proprietary system architecture.

#### Chinese-First Engineering Review, English Localization
Given that the primary Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) were native Chinese-speaking core engineers, we structured our editorial pipeline to write and review in Chinese first. Once the underlying logic, cryptographic details, and system flows passed strict technical verification, we executed high-quality English localization—preserving technical accuracy while maintaining native prose quality.

#### Bridging Technical Abstraction for Institutional Readers
Acting as a bridge between deep cryptographic engineering and executive decision-makers, I translated abstract underlying mechanics (e.g., MPC key share generation, threshold signing, co-signer policies) into intuitive, structured prose, supported by clear system architecture and sequence diagrams.

## Results & Business Value

- **Client Validation:** Feedback during client reviews was overwhelmingly positive. Institutional buyers noted that the manual systematically articulated Cobo's security model and risk parameters, significantly reducing evaluation time and trust barriers during enterprise technical due diligence.
- **Durable Enablement Asset:** Upon publication, Sales, Solutions Engineers, and Compliance teams integrated the manual into customer technical discussions, enterprise contract conversions, and external security audits. It became a foundational technical brand asset for the company.

> **Note:** This case study highlights cross-team project governance, content architecture, and bilingual publication for complex technical systems. Due to security confidentiality and compliance boundaries, proprietary technical schematics and source text are omitted.
